## Question

Kevin flagged the risk that the work was repeatedly renaming the same hole.
The prior LP found that dominant supports `[70,154,286]` failed on three stress
rows, while adding the single tail modulus `130` closed the fixture.  This
audit asks whether that was a stress-row artifact or a genuine algebraic
compression.

## Result

The stronger unit-level identity passes.  The full q286-WBSS unit coefficient
on units modulo `10010` lies in the additive row span generated by total mass
and projections to:

```text
70, 130, 154, 286
```

The unit-level four-modulus relative residual is:

```text
8.752538645134008e-16
```

while the dominant-only family `[70,154,286]` fails with relative residual:

```text
0.025284174723626925
```

Because the unit coefficient descends to this four-modulus additive span, every
reflected target-orbit coefficient

```text
Phi_a({u,a-u}) = (f(u) + f(a-u)) / 2
```

inherits the same four-modulus projection form.  Thus the identity covers all
`5005` even target residues modulo `10010`.  Seven representative target
residues were replayed directly; all seven passed the four-modulus span check,
and all seven failed under dominant-only supports.

## Decision

This is a real narrowing, not just a new label.  Modulus `130` is the missing
projection coordinate for the full q286-WBSS coefficient at the finite
coefficient-algebra level.

The hole still does not close.  The remaining theorem is not a coefficient
identity; it is a prime-distribution statement:

```text
prove strict-central binary-prime projection control for moduli
70, 130, 154, and 286 strong enough to keep <mu_N,Phi_a> positive.
```

Equivalently, prove the raw q286-WBSS signed witness is eventually positive
and finish any finite boundary check.

## Boundary

This audit proves no binary-prime projection theorem, no signed discrepancy
theorem, no q286 threshold theorem, no strict-central Goldbach theorem, and no
Goldbach theorem.  It only shows that the coefficient side has compressed to a
specific four-modulus additive problem.
