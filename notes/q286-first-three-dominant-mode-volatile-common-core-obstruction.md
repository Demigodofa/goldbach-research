# q286 Dominant-Mode Volatile Common-Core Obstruction

Status: finite volatile common-core obstruction only.  This is not a
volatile-rim theorem, stable-core theorem, selected-fixture classifier theorem,
pointwise character-sum estimate, or Goldbach proof.

## Mechanism

The previous threshold ledger records, for each selected row:

```text
margin to floor = stable-core margin + volatile-subset sum
```

This receipt intersects the satisfying volatile-subset families after the
stable core is fixed.  It asks whether the hardest deficit rows share a proper
row-invariant volatile core, or whether the full eight-channel volatile rim is
already forced on the selected fixture.

## Results

The three hardest selected deficit rows by satisfying-subset count are:

```text
13822, 1222142, 164598
```

Their satisfying volatile-subset families have:

```text
intersection count:              1
union count:                    75
Jaccard index:                   0.0133333333
minimum common subset size:      8
proper common subset exists:     false
```

The single common subset is the full eight-channel volatile rim:

```text
(1,1), (1,3), (1,5), (1,7), (2,4), (3,3), (4,4), (4,10)
```

The same is true for all five selected deficit rows: their intersection has
one subset, again the full volatile rim.  The full ten-row selected fixture
also has exactly one common satisfying subset, matching the previous
exact-subset ablation receipt.

The clear side is not the binding obstruction.  All selected clears share
`171` satisfying volatile subsets, with minimum common subset size `0`.

Pairwise deficit overlaps still have proper common subsets:

```text
13822 and 1222142: 2 common subsets, minimum size 6
13822 and 164598:  2 common subsets, minimum size 7
1222142 and 164598: 5 common subsets, minimum size 6
```

The obstruction therefore appears when the three hard deficit obligations are
combined, not in any single pair alone.

## Interpretation

This demotes a small row-invariant volatile-core theorem on the selected
fixture.  The hard rows do not share a proper volatile subset that satisfies
all three thresholds.  The remaining theorem target is row-specific signed
volatile action, a full-package aggregate inequality, or a replacement
arithmetic-placement theorem.

The receipt is derivative finite evidence.  It preserves the exact q286
threshold route, but it does not prove the volatile-rim theorem or Goldbach.
