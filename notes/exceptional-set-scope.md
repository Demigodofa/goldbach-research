# What almost-all and short-interval results can certify

Owner: Kevin's Goldbach investigation. Purpose: check whether published
distribution theorems can remove a named block from the unresolved list.
These are selected scope checks, not a claim of an exhaustive literature
review or of the strongest result as of every future date.

## Global exceptional set

[Pintz, A new explicit formula in the additive theory of primes with
applications II, Theorem1, PDFp.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.09084) states
that the number E(X) of non-Goldbach even integers up to X is less than
X^0.72 beyond an ineffective constant X_2. This is unconditional. The theorem
does not give the identities of possible exceptions or an executable X_2.

For our purpose, even an effective positive upper bound on the number of
exceptions would not by itself identify which targets to remove. A one-element
exceptional set can contain any otherwise unspecified target. A bound strictly
below one, applied to an integer exception count for the requested block,
would certify zero exceptions; this theorem does not supply that bound.

## Almost all inside a shorter interval

[Languasco's institutional publication record and abstract]
(https://research.unipd.it/handle/11577/2465294), for the 2004 paper
*On the exceptional set of Goldbach numbers in short intervals*, gives an
absolute delta>0 with

    |E(X,H)| << H^(1-delta/600),
    H >= X^(7/24+7delta),   H=o(X).

The author abstract is sufficient for this conclusion-scope check; the full
paper was not used to infer effective constants. This again bounds the number
of unnamed exceptions. It does not prove every even target in that interval.

## At least one number in an explicitly short interval

[Chirre and Hagen, On Goldbach numbers in short intervals, Theorem1]
(https://arxiv.org/html/2512.23534v1#S1.Thmtheorem1), posted December2025,
assumes the Riemann Hypothesis and guarantees at least one Goldbach number
in (x,x+123(log x)^2] for every x>=2. Both the constant and the starting
point are explicit; the assumption is conditional, and the conclusion is
existential within the interval. It does not certify all even targets there.

## Consequence for calculation Z

None of these three conclusions alone certifies a named1,000-target block.
They support density statements or a search for one member of the Goldbach
set. The desired elimination requires a pointwise theorem covering every
target, a nonnegative integer exception count proved below one, or a valid
collective certificate with an equivalent conclusion. Collective arithmetic
does not need to print or separately search for a witness for every target.

This is a logical distinction between what the theorems assert. It is not a
claim that nonconstructive proofs are invalid, or that every useful future
certificate must enumerate prime pairs.
