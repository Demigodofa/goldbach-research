# Bounded literature comparison for the local-peak and adaptive-weight results

Owner: Kevin's research; purpose: calibrate novelty before calling a checked
deduction an original contribution to mathematics. Checked 2026-09-08.
This is a bounded comparison, not an exhaustive priority investigation.

Queries included Goldbach representation-function ratios on consecutive
even integers, local maxima and singular series, arbitrarily large ratios,
and neighboring representation counts. Three bodies were inspected:

- Vaughan, *The Hardy-Littlewood Method*, second edition, Chapter 3,
  https://djvu.online/file/y04IjoKF754hT . Its classical global mean-square
  theorem is an explicit dependency of our proofs. Almost-all Goldbach
  asymptotics and singular-series normalization are established ingredients,
  not new results of this investigation.
- Kanchu and Kak, *On Randomness of Goldbach Sequences* (2012),
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.6307 , especially pp.1-3. It discusses local
  peaks associated with products of small primes and statistical dependence
  corrections. Those themes predate this run. The inspected discussion
  does not supply our precise arbitrary-ratio theorem for a fully represented
  fixed-length neighboring block, or the bounded-weight-range obstruction.
  Its statistical statements are not used as proof premises here.
- Bhowmik and Schlage-Puchta, *Mean representation number of integers as
  the sum of primes*, arXiv:0806.3295v3 (2010),
  https://arxiv.org/pdf/0806.3295 , pp.4-5. Their large-value argument uses
  many small prime divisors and averages along multiples of a prime product
  to obtain large von-Mangoldt representation counts. This is meaningful
  prior art for the underlying mechanism. The weighted count includes prime
  powers; their complete mean-value theorem is stated under RH. Our proof
  instead cites Vaughan's unconditional prime-only global theorem and keeps
  its auxiliary modulus fixed.

Conclusion: the association between small-prime divisibility and large
Goldbach counts is not an original discovery here. Rill synthesized the
particular neighbor-domination argument and certificate consequences in
this run, with checked proofs, but this search does not establish that their
precise statements are new to the literature. Keep `new-to-this-task` as
the external novelty label. There is no publication, priority, prize, or
historical-recognition claim. An external review would require a wider
literature check and Kevin's approval before contacting anyone.
